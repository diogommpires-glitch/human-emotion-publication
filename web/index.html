Release details
What MRI can and cannot tell us

Structural imaging does not supply neuron-level parameters. Personal scans are not copied into the app or used to infer cell parameters.

The network has 21 regions (sensory thalamus and cortex, insula, basolateral amygdala threat and reward cells, central amygdala, BNST, hippocampus, three prefrontal areas, accumbens, habenula, hypothalamus, oxytocin cells, PAG, VTA, locus coeruleus, raphe, basal forebrain and brainstem output) with 16,384 to millions of adaptive spiking cells. Glutamate and GABA synapses have AMPA, NMDA, GABA-A and GABA-B receptors, short-term depression and conduction delays. Dopamine, noradrenaline, serotonin, acetylcholine and oxytocin are released in proportion to their source nuclei’s firing, reach their target regions and change excitability through receptor occupancy. Region sizes, wiring strengths and receptor effects are literature-inspired priors, not measured from your scan. The EEG-like traces are a field-potential proxy, not scalp EEG. Network details are in network.json.

An anatomical MRI can help locate large structures, but it does not record neuron spikes or EEG. Functional recordings during a task would be needed to check the timing and size of the simulated responses.
